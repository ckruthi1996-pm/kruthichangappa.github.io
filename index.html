Case study 01

BI Reporting & Observability Platform

Circles.coPlatform Product ManagerTelecom / Analytics — Tsel market

Problem

Operational teams lacked visibility into platform performance, which made it slow to spot and diagnose issues. Decisions were being made without a clear, structured view of what was actually happening.

My role

I worked with business, operations and technology stakeholders to understand reporting needs, defined and prioritized requirements, translated them for engineering, and supported UAT and rollout.

Operational need→Requirements→Dashboard & observability capabilities→Engineering delivery→UAT→Release

Outcome

Introduced BI reporting dashboards and observability capabilities for the Tsel market, giving teams real-time visibility into platform behaviour instead of reacting after the fact.

40% faster decision-making
Reduced issue diagnosis time

Key learning: analytics products need to be designed around decisions and workflows, not just more data. For platform products, observability is only valuable if it helps a team know what to act on.

Case study 02

Customer 360 & Operator Portal

Circles.coPlatform Product ManagerTelecom / Customer Experience — ONIC & AT&T

Problem

Operators had to navigate fragmented workflows to understand customer context, which slowed down service resolution and operational decisions.

My role

I owned product requirements and delivery end to end — defining and prioritizing enhancements, working with UX, engineering and business stakeholders, managing sprint execution, and coordinating rollout across markets.

Solution

Introduced two 360° customer views inside the operator portal, consolidating fragmented information into one place. Alongside this, I shipped platform capabilities including KYC onboarding for AT&T, role-based access control and maker-checker workflows — strengthening governance and compliance.

30% better service resolution efficiency
25% less manual effort across 10,000+ users

Key learning: improving a B2B platform doesn't always mean adding functionality. Bringing the right information together at the right point in the workflow can matter more than building something new.

Case study 05

Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

Circles.coPlatform Product ManagerTelecom / Enterprise SaaS / Platform Governance

Problem

Operator-side users had different responsibilities across markets, but the platform didn't distinguish access by role — making it hard to control who could see or do what, and harder to govern as the platform scaled.

My role

I worked across business, operations and engineering to define and deliver the capability — gathering access and governance requirements, defining user roles and their permissions, prioritizing the work within the platform roadmap, and coordinating rollout across markets.

User→Assigned role→Defined permissions→Accessible platform capabilities

Solution

Introduced Role-Based Access Control across the operator platform, so access followed defined user roles instead of treating every operator user the same way. It was built as part of the broader platform rather than a one-off, market-specific feature — balancing operational flexibility with governance as the platform expanded.

Stronger access governance & permission management
Scalable across multiple markets

Key learning: access control is a product capability, not just a technical implementation. Permissions need to reflect real operational roles and stay scalable as users, markets and platform capabilities grow.
